From a rough sketch to an exact figure

  1. Draw roughly

    Click the joints with the Line tool, about where they belong. Blue means it can still move.

  2. Constrain

    Make lines horizontal, parallel, equal or tangent. The box in the corner counts what is still free.

  3. Dimension and annotate

    Type a length, or a name like a or 2a, and the drawing follows. Then add supports, loads and labels.

  4. Export

    Save TikZ for LaTeX or SVG for slides and Word, at the width your page needs.

TikZ that LaTeX sets in your font

The export is plain TikZ: lines, arcs and nodes you can read. LaTeX typesets the labels, so F in the figure is the same F as in your equations, at the size of your text.

The figure comes out exactly as wide as you set it, with the text at its print size, so what does not overlap on screen does not overlap on paper.

\begin{tikzpicture}[x=1mm, y=1mm, …]
  \draw[member] (0,0) -- (26.76,0);
  \draw[member] (0,0) -- (13.38,20.07);
  \draw[member] (13.38,20.07) -- (40.14,20.07);
  …
  \draw[dim, tip-tip] (0,-10.7) -- (26.76,-10.7);
  \node[label, anchor=north] at (13.38,-10.7) {$a$};
\end{tikzpicture}

It needs \usepackage{tikz} and \usetikzlibrary{arrows.meta, bending}; then \input{truss.tex} where the figure goes.

Questions

Can I change a figure after I have exported it?

Yes. Exported SVG and TikZ files carry the drawing inside them. Open one in Mech Sketch and you get it back with its dimensions and constraints, ready to change.

Do the dimensions have to be real sizes?

No. The first length you dimension sets the scale, and you can name lengths instead of giving numbers: a, 2a, h. The figure is fitted to the width you set either way.

Can I annotate a drawing from my CAD program?

Export it from your CAD program as an SVG and open it in Mech Sketch. Its lines, arcs and curves come in fixed in place, in a sketch of their own, ready for dimensions, fills and labels.

How is it made?

It is mostly vibe-coded: most of the code was written by an AI coding assistant, and a person decided what it should do, tried it out and had it corrected. It is tested as it goes, but treat a figure as you would one from any new tool, and check it before you hand it in.
