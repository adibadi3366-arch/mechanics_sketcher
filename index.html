Welcome to Mech Sketch

Figures for mechanics homework, drawn the way a CAD sketch is: roughly first, exact afterwards, and then into LaTeX.

  1. Draw. On the Sketch tab, pick Line (L) and click to place joints, roughly where they belong. Circles, arcs and beams are there too.
  2. Constrain. Press a constraint such as Horizontal or Equal, then click the geometry it applies to. Blue lines can still move; they turn black once nothing about them can.
  3. Dimension. Press Dimension (D), first among the constraints on the Sketch tab, click a line, middle-click where the dimension should go and type its value. The first one only sets the scale; after that, the drawing follows the number.
  4. Annotate. Supports, loads, moments, axes, notes, fills and hatching are on the Annotate tab.
  5. Check and export. Q shows the figure as it prints. Export saves it as SVG, or as TikZ for LaTeX.
  6. Dynamics. The Dynamics tab has springs and dampers that stretch with what they are tied to, a torsion spring and damper, and the mark of a centre of mass.
  7. Plot. The Plot tab is a workspace of its own for plotting functions, such as an internal force diagram, and curves given by a parameter. A plot is exported as SVG or for pgfplots, and Diagram on the Annotate tab lays one along a beam.

Your sketch is kept in this browser. Save writes it to a file to keep, or to take to another computer: an SVG picture of the figure with the whole drawing inside it. Exported SVG and TikZ files carry the drawing too, so Open brings any of them back to edit. A short tip appears the first time you pick up each tool. The question mark in the top right brings this page back; the speech bubble beside it is for reporting a bug or suggesting something.
